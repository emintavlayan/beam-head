08:45 - Wrote a short development doctrine for Codex and for ourselves
  - File: docs/DOCTRINE.md
  - Lightweight DDD only.
  - Small, well-named modules and domain types.
  - Pure functions and immutable data where practical.
  - Side effects stay at application/infrastructure boundaries.
  - Result<'T, string> for expected failures.
  - FsToolkit.ErrorHandling when Result composition benefits from a computation expression.
  - Avoid speculative abstractions, unnecessary interfaces and unnecessary classes.
  - TrueBeam is first, but TrueBeam should be an implementation detail rather than a reason to make the whole codebase impossible to extend later.
  - We only generalize when a current requirement makes the abstraction useful.
  - Doctrine commit:
    - bbb8a55 - docs: define BeamHead development doctrine

12:30 - Chose one global physical coordinate system
  - Global origin is CAX at the target/source plane:
    - (0, 0, 0)
  - +Z points downstream toward the patient.
  - Isocentre is:
    - (0, 0, 1000 mm)
  - All jaw and MLC poses will ultimately be expressed in this same machine coordinate system.
  - This keeps source divergence, component placement, phase-space location and TOPAS placement understandable in one frame.


12:45 - Chose a common jaw placement reference
  - X and Y jaws use the same kind of local reference point.
  - Jaw reference point:
    - midpoint of the aperture-forming face at the jaw midplane
  - We do not model the mechanical motor, carriage or hinge unless a real need appears.
  - We only need the final rigid-body pose used to generate the STL.
  - This avoids inventing undocumented mechanical pivot locations.


13:00 - Fixed the jaw focusing model around the real source extent
  - TrueBeam jaw divergence should not be treated as if every jaw face focuses to a mathematical point on CAX.
  - Lloyd reports the jaw divergence as projecting back to a 3 x 3 mm square in the target/source plane.
  - For a symmetric field, each jaw edge therefore uses the corresponding source-plane edge:
    - positive side: +1.5 mm
    - negative side: -1.5 mm
  - The field edge at isocentre is connected to the corresponding +/-1.5 mm source-plane edge when calculating jaw position and angle.
  - This matters more to Monte Carlo behaviour than reproducing an undocumented carriage mechanism.


13:10 - Fixed the X-jaw working geometry convention
  - Jaw physical thickness:
    - 78 mm
  - Current literature-derived X-jaw reference/midplane position:
    - Z = 406 mm from source
  - X jaws move linearly and tilt so the aperture-forming face stays focused with the beam divergence.
  - We calculate the final rigid pose directly.
  - We do not invent a physical tilt pivot.


13:20 - Fixed the Y-jaw working geometry convention
  - The reproduced Varian drawing shows:
    - Y-jaw arc trajectory R1 = 28 cm
    - jaw thickness = 3.1 in = 7.8 cm
  - We interpret the 280 mm radius as the radius to the upstream jaw face shown in the drawing.
  - Because our common jaw reference is the midpoint of the aperture-forming face at the jaw midplane:
    - half thickness = 78 / 2 = 39 mm
    - Y-jaw reference radius = 280 + 39 = 319 mm
  - The 39 mm offset is along the jaw local radial direction.
  - It is not a blind +39 mm global-Z shift.
  - Y jaws follow an arc and remain focused while moving.


13:30 - Reduced the first useful TrueBeam output to a fixed 40 x 40 cm field

13:35 - Chose what must be exact and what may be simplified in the first jaw STL
  - Keep exact where currently supported:
    - global coordinate convention
    - isocentre distance
    - source-plane divergence extent
    - jaw thickness
    - X-jaw reference position
    - Y-jaw arc/reference radius
    - jaw focusing direction
    - symmetric +/- jaw placement
  - Outer jaw body dimensions may be simplified if the exact vendor shape is not publicly supported.
  - Any simplified outer dimension must live in one clearly named TrueBeam constant.
  - The code must label it as a modelling approximation.
  - Placement mathematics must not depend on a hidden magic number.
  - We can replace the simplified body shape later without rewriting jaw placement.

Evidence used for today's jaw decisions

  - Samantha A. M. Lloyd, PhD thesis, 2017:
    - "Measurement and Monte Carlo simulation of electron fields for modulated electron radiation therapy"
    - Chapter 6 / jaw-model discussion and Figure 6.2 were used for the TrueBeam jaw trajectory/focusing model.
    - Important point used today: jaw divergence projects back to a 3 x 3 mm square in the target/source plane.

  - Maduka M. Kaluarachchi et al., Medical Physics, 2020:
    - "Validation of a Monte Carlo model for multi leaf collimator based electron delivery"
    - Confirms the different TrueBeam jaw motions:
      - Y jaws follow an arc trajectory.
      - X jaws follow a linear trajectory and tilt to remain focused.
    - Their detailed model used vendor information/CAD for complex head components.

  - Weili Zhong et al., 2022:
    - "Monte Carlo simulation of Cherenkov imaging for Total Skin Electron Treatment with CT DICOM realistic patient geometry"
    - Figure 1 reproduces a Varian treatment-head drawing.
    - The drawing shows:
      - Y-jaw arc trajectory R1 = 28 cm
      - jaw thickness = 3.1 in [7.8 cm]
    - We use those drawing values as the basis for the current Y-jaw radius/thickness convention.

  - Current modelling rule:
    - When public sources give motion/placement information but not the complete vendor CAD body, keep the known placement physics exact and keep unsupported outer-body detail explicitly approximate.


14:24 - Refined the simplified TrueBeam jaw-body dimensions
  - Stronger public evidence supports the 78 mm thickness, X midplane at 406 mm, Y upstream trajectory radius at 280 mm and Y midpoint radius at 319 mm.
  - The 200 mm cross-axis extent and 80 mm closing-axis extent remain explicit modelling approximations because exact public TrueBeam outer jaw dimensions were not found.
